What the research says about order

Training studies that compare the two orders mostly agree on one thing: the order changes how strong your legs get, and not much else.

  • A 2018 meta-analysis in Sports Medicine pooled 10 studies of healthy adults who trained for at least 5 weeks. Doing weights before cardio led to about 7% more gain in lower-body strength. Muscle size, aerobic fitness and body fat did not differ between the orders.
  • Another 2018 meta-analysis, in the Journal of Sports Sciences, found the same pattern: lifting first added about 4 kg to lower-body one-rep max gains, while gains in VO2 max were the same either way.

So the order matters most when leg strength is what you care about, and least when your goal is fat loss or general fitness.

The gap can matter more than the order

In a 7-week study of 58 amateur rugby players, groups did the same strength and cardio work with 0, 6 or 24 hours between them. With no gap, bench press and half-squat strength improved less than with 6 or 24 hours. The 24-hour group also improved aerobic fitness the most. The authors advised leaving at least 6 hours between the two.

A 2022 meta-analysis of 43 studies adds some reassurance: combining cardio with strength training did not reduce muscle growth or maximal strength gains overall. It did reduce gains in explosive strength, and more so when both were done in the same session.

Running and cycling are not the same

A 2012 meta-analysis found that lifting combined with running reduced gains in muscle and strength, while lifting combined with cycling did not. The longer and more often people did cardio, the bigger the reduction. If you lift and run, the type, length and timing of your runs matter more than the order within one session.

Cardio before or after weights, by goal

For fat loss

Body fat changed the same whichever came first, so pick the order you’ll stick with. The exception is hard intervals or running on a leg day: lift first so the weights don’t suffer, because keeping strength up matters while you’re in a calorie deficit.

For muscle and strength

Lift first. If your cardio is long, hard or running, put it on another day or at least 6 hours later.

For endurance

Do your cardio first, since it’s the session that matters most, and keep the lifting after it moderate. The order doesn’t change your aerobic gains.

How the quiz decides

  • Ten minutes or less of easy cardio counts as a warm-up and goes first.
  • Interference load adds up the cardio type (running and hard intervals count most), its length and whether you train legs that day.
  • Strength goal: weights first, or a split if the load is high and your schedule allows it.
  • Endurance goal: cardio first, or a split if the load is high on leg days and your schedule allows it.
  • Fat loss or general fitness: order barely matters, except weights first when hard intervals or running meet a leg day.

Frequently asked questions

Should I do cardio before or after weights to lose fat?

For fat loss, the order makes little difference. A 2018 meta-analysis of 10 training studies found body fat changed the same whether people lifted first or did cardio first. Choose the order you will keep doing, but if your cardio is hard intervals or running and you are training legs, lift first so the weights don’t suffer.

Does cardio kill muscle gains?

Usually not. A 2022 meta-analysis of 43 studies found that adding cardio to strength training did not reduce muscle growth or maximal strength gains. It did reduce gains in explosive strength, such as jumping and sprinting, and more so when both were done in the same session.

How long should I wait between cardio and weights?

If you can split them, aim for at least 6 hours. In a 7-week study of amateur rugby players, doing both with no break led to smaller strength gains than leaving 6 or 24 hours between them, and the 24-hour group improved aerobic fitness the most. Separate days are easiest if your cardio is long or hard.

Does a 5–10 minute warm-up on the treadmill count as cardio first?

No. A few minutes of easy cardio to warm up before lifting is not what the studies on order were testing. They compared full cardio sessions with full lifting sessions. A short, easy warm-up is fine before weights.

Is running or cycling better to combine with lifting?

Cycling interferes less. A 2012 meta-analysis found that combining lifting with running reduced muscle and strength gains, while combining it with cycling did not. Longer and more frequent cardio sessions also made the reduction bigger.
